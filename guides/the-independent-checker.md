# Give a fresh reader the artifact

An author can carry assumptions into a review of its own work.
A fresh reader can expose gaps the author no longer notices.
It can also lack context or make mistakes.

**Prepare the read**

Supply:

- The actual artifact and its scope
- The applicable criteria
- The supporting evidence

Keep the reasoning that produced the artifact out of the review context.
An author's summary can hide the ambiguity the reader needs to find.

Use one reader for the applicable criteria by default.
Split distinct questions when the scope warrants more readers.
A different model can add another perspective when available and appropriate.
Neither a new context nor a different model establishes that a verdict is correct.

**Ask for supported findings**

For each problem, have the reader name:

- The location
- The consequence
- A proposed fix

Rank findings by what a missed problem would cost.

For selection claims, give the reader actual neighboring descriptions and representative requests.
[[review-a-skill]] applies this setup to skills and guides.

**Check the findings**

Treat a finding as a claim to test against the artifact and its evidence.
Resolve confirmed problems.
Explain findings the evidence refutes.
Keep unresolved questions visible.

To check the review, inspect what the reader received.
Check how its findings were handled.
Report when independent reading was unavailable.
